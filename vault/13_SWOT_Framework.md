# 13 — SWOT Analysis Framework

## Overview

Each strategic option must be evaluated against a standardised SWOT framework covering the 11 analysis areas specified in the brief.

## Analysis Areas (from brief)

1. Mine plan physicals & coal quality considerations
2. Infrastructure requirements
3. Equipment suitability, adequacy, and implications for availability & utilisation
4. Geological / geotechnical risk
5. Approvals / environmental risk
6. Carbon liability
7. Deliverability / complexity of the plan
8. Financial implications
9. Labour supply (owner operator vs contract miner)
10. People impact / change management (work process, roster, housing, H&S, training)
11. Other considerations

---

## Option A: Brave Blossom Full Go

### Strengths
- 20-year mine life extension (2033-2052) provides significant cashflow
- Workforce transition from Springbok (skills transferable)
- Defers $900M closure liability by 20+ years
- Gas contract extension possible (methane within specs)
- Community support (extended mine life)
- 75% of mine plan within existing ML (state approval exists)
- Established customer base (Japanese JV members)

### Weaknesses
- Coal quality deterioration → 12% price discount
- ~$389M capital requirement (peak $221M in 2032)
- Working section thins eastward → increasing dilution
- CHPP end-of-life 2032 → requires major capital for extension
- 25% of mine plan on MDL — federal approval uncertain
- No residual equipment from Springbok
- Old workings condition unknown (water, roof stability)

### Opportunities
- Gas monetisation from drainage (revenue + carbon abatement)
- Future targets beyond Brave Blossom (additional projects)
- Potential Bronco Mining acquisition (northern boundary resource)
- Carbon credit generation (ACCU) from gas capture
- Skills pipeline — train workforce during transition period
- Port/rail contract renewal in 2028 — opportunity to secure 6mtpa

### Threats
- Federal approval refusal for MDL portion
- 2× new competing mines on rail line from 2028 (capacity constraint)
- Carbon tax reforms from 2027 (export cost impact)
- Coal market weakness (price decline)
- Geotechnical risk (monocline, faulting, historic roof falls)
- Community backlash if mine closure mismanaged
- Cost escalation on $389M capital (concept estimate → likely +20-30%)
- Private landholder resistance (last 4 panels)

## Option B: No-Go (Closure Only)

### Strengths
- No additional capital required
- Simplest execution path
- No approval risk
- Clear timeline (cessation Q4 2031, relinquishment 2050)
- Risk-free from project execution standpoint

### Weaknesses
- $900M closure liability due 2031-2050 (no revenue to offset)
- Loss of workforce (800+ jobs)
- Town economic collapse (Dustyroo Flats)
- CHPP, infrastructure written off
- Decommissioned mine emissions (20 years, no mitigation revenue)
- Shareholder value destruction

### Opportunities
- Rehabilitation cost optimisation (challenge $900M estimate)
- Progressive rehabilitation acceleration
- Pit void repurposing (water storage, renewables)
- Carbon credit generation from reforestation
- Workforce transition to closure/rehab roles

### Threats
- Regulatory penalties if rehabilitation standards not met
- Carbon liability (Safeguard Mechanism) on decommissioned emissions
- Media backlash (precedent: competing mine company)
- Supplier chain collapse (local diversification already happening)
- Native title holder relations if closure mismanaged

---

## Option C: Phased Go

### Strengths
- Low initial capital ($20M for studies)
- Optionality preserved — go/no-go at each gate
- De-risks federal approval uncertainty before committing $389M
- Maintains workforce engagement during study phase
- Gas contract extension negotiation leverage

### Weaknesses
- Timing risk — delay beyond 2027 may lose workforce
- Inflation/cost escalation during deferral period
- Lost revenue if Springbok closes before Brave Blossom ramps
- Requires bridge funding during gap period
- Less clear strategic narrative for board

### Opportunities
- PFS may reveal better-than-expected geotechnical conditions
- Exploration may identify additional resources
- Market conditions may improve during deferral
- Technology advances may reduce capital costs

### Threats
- Workforce attrition during study phase (contract labour leaves)
- Competing companies may secure rail/port capacity first
- Federal regulatory environment may tighten
- Coal price may decline further, reducing project economics
- Private landholder negotiations may stall

---

## Option D: Bronco Acquisition / Royalty Deal

### Strengths
- Open cut — lower capital, faster start
- Resource similar to historic Brumby (known quality)
- Extends lease boundary resource
- Could bridge cashflow gap between Springbok and Brave Blossom

### Weaknesses
- Bronco has rejected all previous approaches
- Unknown resource quantity/quality in Bronco's lease
- Requires separate acquisition/royalty negotiation
- Potentially smaller scale than Brave Blossom
- Open cut rehabilitation liability

### Opportunities
- Bronco's financial struggles may force a sale
- Royalty model (like Bronco's existing deal) could be template
- Combined open cut + underground optimises infrastructure

### Threats
- Bronco may continue to refuse
- Open cut may face stronger environmental opposition
- Carbon intensity of open cut higher than underground
- Market for thermal/coking blend may be weaker
