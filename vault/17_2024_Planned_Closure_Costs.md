## Key Findings for the Graduate Challenge

### 1. This is the "Valuation Model" Previously Missing
The GC Vault's `11_Outstanding_Information.md` flagged that "valuation model/spreadsheet NOT yet provided." This workbook is that model — it is the 2024 triennial review of the ERC (Estimated Rehabilitation Cost) for Springbok Creek, prepared by WM.

### 2. $900M Liability Composition
- **Direct works**: $494M (54.9%) — the actual rehabilitation activities
- **Contingencies**: $173M (19.2%) — PM, monitoring, and general contingency at 35% combined
- **Holding costs**: $233M (25.9%) — 20 years of site care post-rehabilitation

### 3. Cost Reduction Opportunities for Task 2

**a) Contingency Rate Challenges:**
- 35% combined contingency is applied to the full $494M direct works subtotal
- PM/surveying at 10% = $49.4M — could be challenged if in-house team used
- Environmental monitoring at 10% = $49.4M — increased from prior rate; could be challenged
- General contingency at 15% = $74.1M — increased from prior rate; note says "Detailed Planning (PFS) (2-5 years until mine closure)" — could argue for lower rate if closure deferred via Brave Blossom

**b) Holding Cost Reduction:**
- $233M over 20 years is the second-largest cost component
- **Council Rates** ($112M) — 48% of holding costs. If lease area reduced through progressive relinquishment, this drops significantly
- **Town Maintenance** ($24M) — if town handed to council/government earlier, this cost eliminated
- **Insurance** ($18M) — if risk profile reduces post-closure, premiums should drop
- Deferring closure via Brave Blossom extends revenue and allows progressive cost reduction

**c) Direct Works Opportunities:**
- **Domain 4 (Pits & Voids)** at $216M — the largest single domain. If pit voids can be repurposed (water storage, solar, tailings storage), backfilling volumes and costs reduce significantly
- **Domain 2 (Tailings)** at $126M — if tailings dams are in good condition, the "Ideal 2m" capping rate ($169K/ha) applies rather than "Difficult" ($720K/ha) — a 4.3x difference per hectare
- **House demolition** ($11.8M for 505 houses) — if houses sold/repurposed rather than demolished, this cost is avoided or reduced
- **Dragline demolition** ($4M) — new item in 2024; scrap steel credit of $2.3M already applied

**d) Progressive Rehabilitation:**
- Only $2.5M/yr currently spent on progressive rehab
- Doing more rehabilitation while revenue flows reduces the $494M direct works bill at closure
- Particularly impactful for Domain 6 (Existing Rehab Areas — $23M) where maintenance costs accrue over time

**e) Emission-Related Cost Avoidance:**
- Borehole sealing (Domain 8C — $14.1M) includes gas drainage borehole capping
- If gas drainage continues post-closure for power generation, methane capture creates revenue (carbon credits) rather than being a cost
- Vent shaft sealing ($1.8M) — if sealed with flooding approach rather than dry sealing, fugitive emissions suppressed

### 4. Rate Basis and Assumptions to Challenge
- Many rates include "16% escalation for site specific rates in 2014" — these are 10-year-old escalations
- CHPP demolition is a lump sum ($18.3M rate) — could seek competitive quotes
- Dozer push rates assume WM reference site haul model — actual Springbok conditions may differ
- Topsoil availability assumption: "Due to a lack of topsoil available across site, topsoil will be used over 40% of the area, and biosolids over the remaining 60%" — biosolids approach may be cheaper or more effective
- Load and haul rates assume 40 km/hr equivalent flat haul speed — if actual conditions allow faster cycles, rates are conservative

### 5. 2024 Review Changes (from Comments Column)
Key changes made in the 2024 triennial review:
- **New line items added**: Dragline demolition, water management earthworks/rehab, D11 dam wall pushing, topsoil amelioration with bio-solids, tree/native grass seeding for water management
- **Contingency rates increased**: General contingency from lower rate to 15%; environmental monitoring to 10%
- **Double-counting error corrected**: 2021 review had cells R777-779 double-counted in camp infrastructure
- **Zeroed items**: Many rehabilitation items in Domains 8 and 9 zeroed to consolidate costs into other domains (avoiding double-counting)
- **Rates updated**: Some rates updated based on 2024 WM schedule of rates; others retained from 2014 with 16% escalation

### 6. Implications for Brave Blossom Deferral
