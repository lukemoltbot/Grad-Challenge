### Question 1: "Federal approval is uncertain — why commit $389M before we know if 25% of the mine plan can proceed?"

**Model Answer (DRAFT)**:
We are not committing $389M upfront. Our recommendation is a stage-gated approach where Stage 1 costs only $20M — $10M for PFS and $10M for exploration drilling — over 2027–2028. The full $389M is only committed at Stage Gate 2 (end 2030), after federal approvals are secured. Seventy-five percent of the mine plan sits within the existing Mining Lease where state approval already exists, so even in the worst case of federal refusal, we have a viable ~15-year fallback mine plan. Early engagement with DCCEEW in 2027 de-risks the approval pathway before significant capital is deployed.

**Key supporting data**:
- Stage 1 capital: $20M (PFS + drilling) — 5.1% of total $389M
- Stage Gate 2 (end 2030): federal approvals must be secured before Stage 3 capital ($266M) is committed
- Fallback: 75% within ML = ~15-year mine life (vs 20-year with MDL)
- **[DRAFT]** Fallback NPV: ~$748M (75% of $997M) — still strongly positive

### Question 4: "The $900M closure estimate — how reliable is it, and can you actually reduce it?"

**Model Answer (DRAFT)**:
The $900M is a third-party estimate using the QLD ERC tool, based on WM's 2024 unit rate library (296 line items) across 9 closure domains. Our workbook audit identified six specific, line-item-level reduction opportunities totalling approximately $180.3M (20%): a duplicate TSF costing of $39.5–43.8M, contingency reduction from 35% to 25% saving $49.4M, house sale vs demolition saving $11.8M, accelerated progressive rehab saving $11.2M, progressive lease relinquishment saving ~$50M, and gas drainage monetisation converting a $14.1M cost into revenue. Critically, if Brave Blossom proceeds, the entire $900M is deferred 20+ years to post-2052 — at a 7% discount rate, the NPV saving is approximately $508M, which exceeds the $322.9M PV of capital (at 8%) by ~$185M.

**Key supporting data**:
- $900M composition: $494M direct works (54.9%) + $173M contingency (19.2%) + $233M holding (25.9%)
- Largest domain: Pits & Voids at $216.5M (24.1%)
- Holding costs: $233M over 20 years; council rates dominate at $112M (48%)
- SMART reductions: ~$180.3M (20%) — 6 specific measures (vault file 20)
- NPV of deferral: ~$508M at 7% DR (direct + contingency only; holding costs shift but same total)
- Capital-to-liability ratio: $1 capital defers $2.31 of closure liability
- Net positive NPV before Brave Blossom revenue: ~$185M ($508M closure deferral PV at 7% minus $322.9M capital PV at 8%)

### Hostile Question 1: "Federal approval is uncertain — why are we committing $389M before we know if 25% of the mine plan can proceed?"

The full $389M is only committed at Stage Gate 2, end of 2030, after federal approvals are secured or a clear pathway is confirmed. If approval is refused, we lose $20M, not $389M. Furthermore, 75% of the mine plan sits within the existing Mining Lease where state approval already exists — the fallback is a ~15-year mine life, still strongly NPV-positive. The real question isn't why we're committing before approval — it's whether we can afford *not* to start the approval process now, given that the 2028 rail/port renewal window and Springbok's 2031 cessation date are both fixed."

### Hostile Question 2: "Thermal coal is in structural decline — why invest in a new underground mine now?"

Second, even within coking coal, we're not betting on rising prices — our model uses a PHCC benchmark of USD $240/t, which is below the historical range of $295–360/t. The NPV of $997M is built on conservative pricing. The 12% quality discount means our realised price is $211.20/t, and the project is still strongly positive. The Japanese JV customers — who are steelmakers, not power generators — have indicated steady demand for the product. We're not making a contrarian bet on coal markets; we're investing in a 20-year extension of an existing operation with known customers, known geology, and existing infrastructure."

**Key data to cite**:
- Product: 100% PHCC (hard coking coal) — steelmaking input, not thermal
