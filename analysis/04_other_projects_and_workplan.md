## 4A. Brumby Open Cut Development Project (Tasks 4.1–4.3)

### 4.1 Strategic Review

#### Background — Dual Status of Brumby

The Brumby open cut has two distinct phases that must be understood by the board:

1. **Historic operation** (2011–2015): Previously produced a 70/30 thermal-to-coking coal split. Reserves depleted within the existing Mining Lease (ML). The resource extends beyond the ML boundary into the neighbouring lease (ML1654, held by Bronco Mining).
2. **Development project** (new — per the 8-page supplemental brief): A proposed new open cut targeting the **Xanadu seam** in an adjacent tenement, 25 km northeast of the existing Springbok operation.

This dual status means Brumby is not merely a depleted legacy asset — it represents a **viable development target** with defined parameters, not yet assessed beyond concept stage.

#### Project Parameters (from 8-Page Supplemental Brief)

| Attribute | Detail | Source |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Location | 25 km northeast of Springbok Coal Complex | File 15 / File 16 |
| Tenement | Adjacent tenement (not the Springbok ML) | File 15 |
| Target seam | Xanadu seam | File 15 |
| Strip ratio | Low (historic Brumby OC: 4.5–4.9 bcm/t) | File 21 (Historical BrumbyOC sheet) |
| Target production | 1.5 Mtpa ROM | File 15 |
| Product split | 70/30 GCN thermal / Premium Hard Coking Coal | File 15 |
| Status | Development / concept stage | File 15 |
| Historical benchmark | ROM 1.0–1.35 Mtpa, yield 69–75%, GCN energy 6,212–6,290 kcal/kg (gar), ash 9.2–10.1% | File 21 (Historical Performance BrumbyOC) |

#### Strategic Significance

Brumby Xanadu represents a **potential third life-extension option** alongside Brave Blossom (Option A) and the "do nothing" scenario (Option B). Its strategic value lies in several dimensions:

1. **Cashflow bridge potential**: A 1.5 Mtpa open cut could bridge the cashflow gap between Springbok closure (Q4 2031) and Brave Blossom first coal (~2033). Even a 2-year bridging operation at 1.5 Mtpa would generate material revenue during the gap period.

2. **Lower capital intensity**: Open cut operations typically require lower upfront capital and faster ramp-up than underground longwall operations. The low strip ratio (historical benchmark 4.5–4.9 bcm/t) supports this thesis. By comparison, Brave Blossom requires ~$389M capital with first coal in ~2033.

3. **Known geological analog**: The Xanadu seam is in the same structural basin as historic Brumby operations (2011–2015), with benchmark data available on strip ratio, yield, and coal quality. This de-risks geological uncertainty relative to a greenfield target.

4. **Product diversification**: The 70/30 GCN thermal / PHCC split provides product diversification, but also introduces **thermal coal price exposure**. Thermal coal (GCN 6000 NAR at $118/t forecast) is more volatile and lower-priced than Premium HCC ($240/t forecast). At 70% thermal, revenue is heavily weighted to the lower-margin product.

5. **Lease/access complexity**: The resource extends beyond the Springbok ML into Bronco Mining's lease (ML1654). Any development may require land/access negotiation with Bronco — creating a strategic link to Option D (Bronco Acquisition). Whether the Xanadu seam in the adjacent tenement is the same seam extending into Bronco's lease remains an open question.

#### Strategic Recommendation (Concept-Level)

Brumby Xanadu should be **advanced to pre-feasibility study (PFS) stage** as a parallel workstream to Brave Blossom. The project offers a potentially lower-capital, faster-ramp alternative that could serve as either:
- A **bridge** between Springbok closure and Brave Blossom first coal, or
- A **complementary operation** running in parallel with Brave Blossom, providing product diversification (thermal coal revenue stream).

However, the lack of resource tonnage, capital estimates, and confirmed tenement status means the project cannot yet be valued. PFS funding of ~$5–8M (exploration drilling + concept study) is recommended to resolve the critical data gaps.

## 4B. Bronco Acquisition / Royalty — Option D (Tasks 4.4–4.6)

### 4.4 Strategic Review

#### Background

Bronco Mining holds ML1654, which shares a **northern lease boundary** with the Springbok Coal Complex. Bronco operates a **small open cut** mine and has a **royalty-based extraction deal** with a neighbouring mine. WM has approached Bronco to purchase their lease **multiple times** — all approaches have been rejected.

The strategic rationale for Option D is that the Springbok coal resource **extends beyond the Springbok ML into Bronco's lease** at a similar strip ratio and coal quality. Acquiring Bronco's lease (or negotiating a royalty/access deal) would unlock this resource extension.

#### Key Facts (from Vault Files 05, 11, 13)

| Attribute | Detail | Source |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Lease holder | Bronco Mining (ML1654) | File 05 |
| Location | Shares northern lease boundary with Springbok ML | File 05 |
| Operation type | Small open cut, currently operating | File 05 |
| Resource quality | "Similar to what was historically mined" (i.e., similar to historic Brumby) | File 11 |
| Strip ratio | Similar to Springbok ML boundary (implied similar to historic Brumby: 4.5–4.9 bcm/t) | File 05 |
| Previous approaches | WM approached to purchase — rejected multiple times | File 05 |
| Existing deal model | Royalty-based extraction deal with a neighbouring mine | File 05 |
| Resource extension | Springbok resource extends beyond ML into Bronco's lease | File 05 |

#### Strategic Rationale

1. **Resource extension**: The Springbok coal resource continues into Bronco's lease at similar quality and strip ratio. Acquiring or accessing this resource extends Springbok's mine life without developing a new standalone operation.

2. **Bridge cashflow**: A Bronco-based open cut could bridge the cashflow gap between Springbok closure (2031) and Brave Blossom first coal (~2033), similar to the Brumby thesis but potentially faster to execute if the lease is acquired.

3. **Combined infrastructure**: If Bronco's lease is acquired, the open cut could potentially use existing Springbok infrastructure (CHPP, TLO, rail/port contracts) — unlike Brumby (25 km away), Bronco is adjacent.

4. **Royalty model alternative**: If Bronco continues to reject a full acquisition, a royalty-based extraction deal (similar to Bronco's existing arrangement with its neighbour) could provide access without a full lease purchase. This reduces WM's capital commitment but also reduces revenue share.

5. **Known resource quality**: Resource is described as "similar to what was historically mined" at Brumby — implying a 70/30 thermal/HCC split with comparable yield and quality. This provides a benchmark, though specific tonnage and quality data for Bronco's lease are not available.

#### Key Gaps (from Vault File 11 — 🟢 Lower Priority)

| Gap | Detail | Impact |
|-----|--------|--------|
| Resource quantity/quality in Bronco's lease | No tonnage estimate, no specific coal quality data | Cannot value the acquisition |

#### Strategic Recommendation (Concept-Level)

Option D should be maintained as a **strategic optionality** — WM should continue to monitor Bronco's financial position and maintain a relationship, but should not commit capital to an acquisition that has been repeatedly rejected. If Bronco's financial circumstances change (as suggested in File 13), WM should be positioned to move quickly.

A **dual-track approach** is recommended:
- **Track 1**: Maintain periodic approach to Bronco for acquisition or royalty deal
- **Track 2**: If Brumby Xanadu PFS confirms the seam extends into Bronco's lease, the case for acquisition strengthens (resource continuity argument)

## 4C. Future Exploration — Option E (Tasks 4.7–4.8)

### 4.7 Strategic Review

#### Background

Option E represents the scenario where WM **defers Brave Blossom development** and instead invests in **low-cost exploration** to identify new coal targets beyond the currently defined resources. This is the most speculative of the five options — it bets on finding new resources rather than developing known ones.

#### Project Parameters (from Vault File 12)

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Description | Defer Brave Blossom, assess new exploration targets |
| Capital | $5–10M (exploration only) |
| Revenue extension | Post-2052 (very long lead time) |
| Closure impact | $900M due 2031–2050 (no deferral) |
| Risk level | Medium |

#### Strategic Rationale

1. **Low capital entry**: At $5–10M, the exploration budget is a fraction of Brave Blossom's $389M capital requirement. This preserves capital for other strategic purposes (closure liability reduction, Bronco acquisition, shareholder returns).

2. **Portfolio optionality**: Historic mining in the region suggests further coal targets exist (vault file 08). Exploration maintains WM's pipeline of future projects without committing to any single option.

3. **Deferral of major capital**: By not committing to Brave Blossom ($389M), WM avoids the largest capital risk in the portfolio. Capital can be deployed when market conditions, carbon regulations, and approval pathways are clearer.

4. **Technology and market deferral**: Waiting allows WM to benefit from technology advances (autonomous mining, electric equipment, carbon capture), potentially lower capital costs, and clearer market signals on long-term coal demand.

5. **Closure liability remains due**: The critical trade-off — $900M closure liability falls due 2031–2050 with no revenue to offset. This is the most significant financial weakness of Option E.

#### Relationship to Other Options

Option E is the **exploration-only pathway** — it does not generate revenue until post-2052 at the earliest. It is most appropriately viewed as:
- A **fallback** if Options A and D are not viable
- A **parallel activity** that can be run alongside any other option (exploration is always worthwhile)
- A **deferral strategy** that buys time but incurs the $900M closure cost

#### Strategic Recommendation (Concept-Level)

Option E should **not** be recommended as a standalone strategy — the $900M closure liability due 2031–2050, combined with no revenue extension before 2052, makes this financially destructive in the near-to-medium term. However, **exploration funding of $5–10M should be included as a parallel workstream** within the forward workplan, regardless of which primary option is selected. Exploration is low-cost, maintains pipeline optionality, and could identify the next Brave Blossom.

3. **Option D (Bronco) is complementary, not substitutive** — it does not replace Brave Blossom but could bridge the 2031–2033 cashflow gap or run in parallel. Its value is constrained by Bronco's refusal to sell and the lack of resource data.

4. **The capital-to-liability ratio is the key strategic lever** — investing $389M in Brave Blossom defers $900M of closure liability (0.43:1 ratio, or $2.31 deferred per $1 invested). This is a compelling return before counting any project revenue.

5. **Option E should be a parallel activity, not a standalone strategy** — exploration funding of $5–10M should be included in the forward workplan regardless of the primary option selected.
