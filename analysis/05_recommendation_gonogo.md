## Executive Summary — Lead Recommendation

**The Board should authorise a stage-gated progression of the Brave Blossom underground development, commencing with a $20M Pre-Feasibility Study and exploration program in 2027.**

This recommendation is not a blank cheque for the full ~$389M capital commitment. It is a disciplined, gate-controlled pathway that commits only $20M of initial capital, defers the decision to proceed to major development until the PFS confirms technical and commercial viability, and — critically — defers a $900M closure liability by 20+ years while preserving optionality at every stage.

The core financial logic is compelling and robust to assumption changes: **every $1 invested in Brave Blossom defers $2.31 of closure liability** ($900M ÷ $389M). Even before any Brave Blossom revenue is counted, the net present value of liability deferral alone is approximately **~$185M net positive NPV** (NPV of closure deferral $508M at 7% discount, minus PV of capital $322.9M at 8% — see Rec 1 cashflow analysis for the derivation; an earlier vault estimate on a different calculation basis was ~$106M). Revenue from ~20 years of hard coking coal production at ~6mtpa saleable then builds on that base, producing confirmed project NPVs of **~$997M (Brave Blossom standalone)** and **~$1,277M (Combined with Springbok residual)**. Project IRR is estimated at **~52%** (concept-stage estimate, pending PFS refinement).

Two complementary recommendations accompany the primary decision:

1. **Optimise the $900M closure liability** via SMART reduction measures — targeting a ~20% reduction (~$180.3M saving) on the third-party estimate.
2. **Implement carbon abatement & gas monetisation** — converting a 20-year post-closure emissions liability into ACCU revenue and gas sales.

Together, these three recommendations transform a passive, deteriorating position (Option B: "Do Nothing") into an active value-creation portfolio that retains 800+ jobs, maintains community social licence, and converts closure and carbon liabilities into managed, revenue-generating obligations.

## Task 5.1 — "Do Nothing" (Option B) Scenario Analysis

### The Consequence of Inaction

Option B — the "No-Go" or "Do Nothing" scenario — is the baseline against which all alternatives must be measured. It is superficially attractive (zero additional capital, no approval risk, straightforward execution) but represents the most value-destructive path available to the Board when assessed over a 20-year horizon.

#### Financial Consequences

| Parameter | Option B: Do Nothing |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Closure liability | **$900M** due 2031–2050 (no revenue to offset) |
| Revenue post-2031 | **$0** (Springbok ceases Q4 2031) |
| Additional capital | $0 (but full $900M closure cost incurred) |
| Net cash position 2031–2050 | Deeply negative — $900M outflow with no offsetting revenue |
| Carbon liability | Unmanaged 20-year decommissioned mine emissions |
| Asset write-offs | CHPP, infrastructure, TLO written off at cessation |

The $900M closure estimate — derived from the Queensland ERC tool by a third party — is "considerably higher than anticipated at purchase." Under Option B, this liability crystallises in full against zero revenue, representing a direct destruction of shareholder value.

#### Workforce & Community Consequences

- **800+ jobs lost** at cessation (Q4 2031), including both staff and contractors.
- **Dustyroo Flats** — the town is nearly entirely dependent on the mining complex. Closure triggers economic collapse of the township.
- A **competing mine company** that recently closed and abandoned its town faced severe media backlash — establishing a clear precedent for reputational damage.
- **Supplier chain collapse**: local suppliers are already diversifying in anticipation; lead times are increasing.

#### Carbon & Regulatory Consequences

- Decommissioned mine emissions continue for **20 years** post-cessation with no mitigation revenue under Option B.
- The site remains subject to the **Safeguard Mechanism** — facilities emitting >100,000 tonnes CO₂-e annually are regulated; decommissioned mine emissions are a defined Scope 1 source.
- Regulatory penalties apply if rehabilitation standards are not met, and the relinquishment timeline (target 2050) carries ongoing compliance obligations.

#### Option B SWOT (from Vault File 13)

| | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|-----------|------------|
| • | No additional capital required | $900M closure liability due 2031–2050 (no revenue offset) |
| • | Simplest execution path | Loss of workforce (800+ jobs) |
| • | No approval risk | Town economic collapse (Dustyroo Flats) |
| • | Clear timeline (cessation Q4 2031, relinquishment 2050) | CHPP, infrastructure written off |
| • | Risk-free from project execution standpoint | Decommissioned mine emissions (20 yrs, no mitigation revenue) |
| | | Shareholder value destruction |

| | Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---------------|---------|
| • | Rehabilitation cost optimisation (challenge $900M estimate) | Regulatory penalties if rehab standards not met |
| • | Progressive rehabilitation acceleration | Carbon liability (Safeguard Mechanism) on decommissioned emissions |
| • | Pit void repurposing (water storage, renewables) | Media backlash (precedent: competing mine company) |
| • | Carbon credit generation from reforestation | Supplier chain collapse (local diversification already happening) |
| • | Workforce transition to closure/rehab roles | Native title holder relations if closure mismanaged |

#### Verdict on Option B

Option B is the **default failure mode**, not a viable strategic choice. While it carries no project execution risk, it imposes the full $900M closure cost with no revenue, destroys 800+ jobs, collapses a dependent township, and leaves a 20-year unmanaged carbon liability. The SWOT opportunities listed above (cost optimisation, pit repurposing, reforestation credits) are all **also available under Recommendations 1–3** — but under Option B they are the *only* mitigants, pursued without the benefit of continued revenue to fund them. The opportunities in Option B's SWOT are, in effect, subsumed as active workstreams in the recommended path.

**Conclusion: Option B is rejected as a standalone strategy. Its consequence — $900M outflow, no revenue, 800+ jobs lost, unmanaged carbon — is unacceptable.**

### Recommendation 1: Stage-Gated Brave Blossom Development

**Status**: CONFIRMED — cashflow figures updated (capital-inclusive NPV basis, Tasks 1.3, 1.7)

#### Rationale

Brave Blossom is a coal development project (underground) acquired as part of the original three-asset JV package. It offers a ~20-year mine life extension (2033–2052) producing ~6mtpa saleable Premium Hard Coking Coal from a 300m-wide longwall designed for 8mtpa ROM throughput. The development:

- **Defers the $900M closure liability** by 20+ years (to post-2052) — the single largest financial lever available.
- **Retains 800+ staff and contractors** via workforce transition from Springbok (skills transferable).
- **Maintains community social licence** — the town of Dustyroo Flats remains economically viable.
- **75% of the mine plan lies within the existing Mining Lease** (state approval already in place); only 25% on the MDL requires federal approval.
- **Established customer base** — Japanese JV members provide steady demand for hard coking coal.

The recommendation is **not** an upfront commitment of the full ~$389M capital. It is a **stage-gated** approach that commits only $20M initially, with three formal gates before full capital deployment. This structure directly addresses the principal weakness of a "Full Go" — committing $389M before technical and regulatory uncertainties are resolved.

#### Resultant Cashflow Analysis — CONFIRMED

The financial case for Brave Blossom rests on two compounding value drivers:

**Value Driver 1 — Closure Liability Deferral (the "insurance" value)**

The ~$185M net positive NPV represents the present value of pushing the $900M outflow from 2031–2050 to post-2052, **before any Brave Blossom revenue is counted**. The calculation is: NPV of closure deferral ($508M at a 7% discount rate) minus the present value of capital invested ($322.9M at an 8% discount rate) = ~$185M net positive. An earlier vault estimate cited ~$106M on a different calculation basis; both figures are retained for transparency. This means the deferral alone — the mere act of keeping the mine complex operating — creates value independent of whether Brave Blossom itself is profitable. ### Go/No-Go Position

**The recommendation is GO — but a gated Go, not an unconditional Go.**

- **GO** on authorising $20M for Stage 1 (PFS + drilling + approval engagement + closure review + carbon study) in 2027–2028.
- **GO** on commencing closure liability optimisation and carbon abatement workstreams immediately (end-2027 implementation target).
- **DEFER** the decision on major capital ($104M Stage 2, $265M Stage 3) to Gate 1 (end 2028), Gate 2 (end 2030), and Gate 3 (2032) respectively.

This approach commits minimal capital now, funds the studies that will de-risk the concept-stage estimate, preserves No-Go optionality at every gate, defers $2.31 of closure liability for every $1 invested, and — if the gates pass — delivers a 20-year revenue extension on a ~$997M NPV base (IRR ~52% estimated).
