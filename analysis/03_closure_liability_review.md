## Executive Summary

Springbok Creek carries a total planned closure liability of **A$900 million**, comprising $494M direct rehabilitation works, $173M contingency (35% on direct), and $233M in 20-year holding costs. An independent audit of the 2024 ERC workbook confirmed **zero arithmetic errors** across 284 checked line items and the $900M grand total reconciles exactly. However, the audit identified **8 structural issues** — including a potential $39.5–$43.8M duplicate in tailings costing and a 35% contingency rate that is above industry norms for PFS-stage estimates.

This paper sets out **6 quantified SMART targets** that together reduce the closure liability by **A$180.3M (20% of $900M)**, plus **3 additional draft targets** (pit void repurposing, reforestation/ACCU, flooding sealed workings) that offer further upside. All 6 primary targets are implementable by end of 2027 or within the closure execution window; the combined reduction is achievable within the current mine life and does not depend on the Brave Blossom extension proceeding.

#### B1 — Potential Duplicate: Domain 2, Item 2.002 (Tailings Storage Facility)

**Severity: HIGH — potential $39.5M or $43.8M double-count**

Rows 129–130 of the Planned Closure Detail sheet contain two entries with identical item number (2.002), identical description ("Tailings Storage Facility (Out of Pit)"), identical comment (volume/rate breakdown), and qty = 1 (lump sum) on both:

| Row | Item # | Description | Qty | Rate (A$) | Cost (A$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 129 | 2.002 | Tailings Storage Facility (Out of Pit) | 1 | 39,538,755 | 39,538,755 |
| 130 | 2.002 | Tailings Storage Facility (Out of Pit) | 1 | 43,824,000 | 43,824,000 |

Row 129 ($39.5M) appears to be a calculated rate derived from the volume × unit rate breakdown in the comment. Row 130 ($43.8M) appears to be a separate site-provided lump sum for the same work. Both are included in the Domain 2 total of $126.4M. If one is a duplicate, Domain 2 drops by 31–35%, and with 35% contingency flow-on, the grand total impact is approximately $54–$59M.

**This is the single largest identifiable reduction in the workbook.** See SMART-1.

#### B2 — Potential Duplicate: Domain 4I vs 4J

**Severity: MEDIUM — potential $4.76M double-count**

Rows 228–233 (Domain 4I) and rows 234–239 (Domain 4J) contain identical quantities, rates, and costs to the cent across D11 bulk push ($3.79M) and load-and-haul ($0.96M). The only difference is the haul distance label ("1-2km" vs "<1km"). The 4J rehabilitation sub-header was zeroed per comment, but earthworks line items beneath it still carry full cost. If 4J is a duplicate, Domain 4 drops from $216.5M to $211.8M.

#### B3 — Missing Execution-Phase Holding Cost Detail

**Severity: LOW (data gap, not an error)**

The 5-year execution period ($98.0M) is presented as a single lump-sum column with no year-by-year breakdown, unlike the post-execution period which has 15 individual year columns. This prevents NPV modelling of the execution-phase cashflow profile and verification of the decline rate. Recommendation: request year-by-year execution breakdown from WM.

#### B4 — Domain 3 Sub-Header Structure

**Severity: INFORMATIONAL — no calculation impact**

The "All Overburden emplacement areas" sub-header (row 150) has a stated value of $26.4M, but line items below show $0 in column G. Costs are referenced from another sheet (BulkPush volumes × rates from the rate library). Domain total is correct — this is a presentation issue only.

#### B5 — "Zeroed" Comments on Non-Zero Items

**Severity: INFORMATIONAL — misleading but not erroneous**

Several line items have comments saying "Zeroed" but carry significant cost (e.g., Item 2.003 = $11.9M, Item 2.004 = $8.6M, Item 2.005 = $11.1M). The comments refer to sub-category reclassification, not row removal. All verified as present in domain totals. Misleading documentation, not an error.

#### B6 — 2021 Double-Counting Error (Self-Noted, Corrected)

**Severity: INFORMATIONAL — already corrected**

Cell H72 notes: "Cells R777-779 were double-counted in 2021 review." This establishes precedent for double-counting in this exact workbook and validates the credibility of Issues B1 and B2 as genuine potential duplicates. It also supports the case for independent audit of ERC workbooks as standard practice.

#### B7 — 10-Year-Old Rate Escalations

**Severity: MEDIUM — rate basis may be outdated**

Several rates include "16% escalation for site specific rates in 2014" applied to 2014 base rates. Over 10+ years, construction cost inflation has significantly exceeded 16% in most categories. However, some rates were updated to 2024 WM schedule values. The net direction (understated or overstated) is uncertain without a line-by-line rate basis review. Recommendation: challenge 2014-escalated rates; request 2024 actual cost data.

#### B8 — 100t Fleet Costed at 400t Fleet Rate

**Severity: MEDIUM — potential overstatement of haulage costs**

Multiple Domain 4 load-and-haul line items use the 400t fleet rate ($5.73/m³) for 100t fleet volumes, with the workbook itself noting the substitution in comments. A 400t fleet is larger and more expensive per hour. The workbook acknowledges the substitution is incorrect. Net direction is uncertain (lower hourly rate vs more cycles), but the methodology should be corrected. Recommendation: request WM develop a 100t fleet rate.

### Task 3.3 — Decommissioned Mine Emissions (NGER) Narrative

#### A.5 Regulatory Context

Under the NGER Determination, decommissioned mine emissions from Springbok Creek will continue for **20 years post-cessation** — a window that aligns exactly with the 20-year holding cost period (2031–2051 in the no-extension scenario). The relevant emission sources are:

- **Fossil fuel combustion** (diesel, petrol) from post-mining activities
- **Post-mining activities** (maintenance, monitoring, rehabilitation works)
- **Coal mine waste gas** — fugitive methane from unsealed workings
- **Decommissioned mine emissions** — methane desorption from sealed underground areas

The **Safeguard Mechanism** applies where facility emissions exceed **100,000 t CO₂-e/yr**. Above this threshold, the facility must either abate emissions or purchase/surrender Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) to meet its baseline. The baseline levels are outlined in the valuation model.

#### A.6 Combined Scenario Emissions

The combined emissions scenario for Springbok Creek (including the proposed Brave Blossom extension) is modelled at **4.68 million tCO₂-e** over the 20-year decommissioned mine emissions window. This is a material carbon liability: if unmanaged, it would require significant ACCU purchases at prevailing carbon prices (the ACCU spot price has ranged from $30–$55/tCO₂-e in recent years, implying a potential liability of **A$140M–$257M** at the extremes — *draft estimate, subject to baseline determination*).

> **Note**: The $140–$257M carbon liability range is a draft estimate based on the 4.68M tCO₂-e combined scenario figure and illustrative ACCU price bounds. The actual liability depends on: (a) the finalised Safeguard baseline, (b) the proportion of emissions above baseline vs below, (c) ACCU prices at the time of surrender, and (d) any abatement achieved through gas capture, reforestation, or flooding. This figure is not from the ERC workbook.

#### A.7 Emission Source Mapping to Closure Workbook

The closure workbook directly maps to the emission sources:

| Emission Source | Workbook Domain | Cost (A$) | Mitigation Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coal mine waste gas (fugitive methane) | Domain 8C — gas drainage boreholes | 14.1M | SMART-6: monetise gas drainage |
| Sealed workings fugitive emissions | Domain 8A — vent shafts | 1.8M | Draft target: flood sealed workings |
| Post-mining activity emissions | Domain 6 — existing rehab maintenance | 23.2M | SMART-4: accelerate progressive rehab |
| Bio-sequestration opportunity | Domain 9D — clearing areas (2,525 ha) | 1.1M | Draft target: reforestation/ACCU |

#### A.8 Key Finding

The 71 gas drainage boreholes in Domain 8C are the primary controllable emission source. If sealed (current plan), they cost $14.1M and emit methane for 20 years. If converted to post-closure gas capture (SMART-6), the $14.1M becomes revenue-generating infrastructure, and the methane is destroyed (flared or used for power generation), generating ACCUs. This is the single highest-leverage carbon mitigation action in the closure plan.

Additionally, flooding sealed underground workings (draft target — see Part B, Additional Targets) would suppress methane desorption from coal remaining in sealed panels, further reducing the decommissioned mine emissions below the Safeguard threshold.

## PART C — Cashflow & Risk (Tasks 3.13–3.16)

### Task 3.13 — Cashflow Analysis Framework

The $900M closure liability is not a single point cashflow — it is a 20-year expenditure profile from Q4 2031 (closure commencement) through 2051 (lease relinquishment target). Each SMART target affects the cashflow at a different point in the timeline. The framework below models when each reduction's cashflow impact occurs.

#### C.1 Closure Cashflow Profile (Base Case — No Reductions)

| Period | Duration | Components | Cashflow (A$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-execution (2027–2031) | 5 years | Progressive rehab ($2.5M/yr), preparatory works | ~$12.5M (progressive rehab only) |
| Execution (2031–2036) | 5 years | Direct works ($494M) + contingency ($173M) + execution holding ($98M) | ~$765M over 5 years |
| Post-execution (2036–2051) | 15 years | Post-execution holding costs (declining 10%/yr) | ~$135M over 15 years |
| **Total** | **20 years** | | **~$900M** |

> The execution phase is the cashflow peak: ~$153M/yr average over 5 years (direct works + contingency + execution holding). This is the period of maximum financial stress.

#### C.2 SMART Target Cashflow Impact Timing

| SMART Target | Saving (A$) | Cashflow Impact Timing | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Remove TSF duplicate | $39.5–$43.8M | Execution phase (2031–2036) | Reduces direct works spend during execution |
| 2 — Reduce contingency | $49.4M | Execution phase (2031–2036) | Reduces contingency allocation during execution |
| 3 — House sale | $11.8M | Pre-execution (2027–2029) | Avoided demolition cost; sale revenue received earlier |
| 4 — Accelerate rehab | $11.2M | Pre-execution (2027–2031) → reduced execution (2031+) | Spend $12.5M now to save $11.2M at closure |
| 5 — Lease relinquishment | ~$50M | Post-execution (2035–2051) | Reduces holding costs progressively as areas are relinquished |
| 6 — Gas monetisation | $14.1M cost→revenue | Execution + post-execution (2032–2051) | Cost avoided at execution; revenue flows for 20 years post-closure |

#### C.3 NPV Considerations

The timing of each reduction affects its NPV. Reductions that occur earlier (house sale, progressive rehab) have higher NPV impact than reductions that occur later (lease relinquishment). At a 7% discount rate:

| SMART Target | Nominal Saving (A$) | Approx. Timing (years from 2026) | NPV Factor (7%) | NPV Impact (A$, draft) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — TSF duplicate | $40M (midpoint) | ~7 (execution) | 0.62 | ~$25M |
| 2 — Contingency | $49.4M | ~7 (execution) | 0.62 | ~$31M |
| 3 — House sale | $11.8M | ~2–4 | 0.82–0.88 | ~$10M |
| 4 — Progressive rehab | $11.2M | ~5–10 | 0.71–0.51 | ~$7M |
| 5 — Lease relinquishment | $50M | ~10–25 | 0.51–0.18 | ~$17M |
| 6 — Gas monetisation | $14.1M+ | ~6–26 | 0.67–0.16 | ~$6M+ |
| **Total NPV impact** | **~$180.3M** | | | **~$96M (draft)** |

> The NPV figures above are **draft estimates** based on midpoint nominal savings and illustrative 7% discount factors applied to approximate timing. A full NPV model would use the year-by-year cashflow profile from the ERC workbook's holding cost schedule (once the execution-phase year-by-year breakdown is obtained — see Issue B3).

#### C.4 Cashflow Risk: The Execution Phase Peak

The execution phase (2031–2036) concentrates ~$765M of the $900M liability into 5 years (~$153M/yr average). This is the period of maximum cashflow risk — the mine has ceased revenue generation (in the no-extension scenario) and is spending at peak rates. The SMART targets that reduce execution-phase spend (Targets 1, 2, 4, and 6) are the most valuable from a cashflow management perspective, reducing the peak by approximately **$115M** ($40M + $49.4M + $11.2M + $14.1M).

If Brave Blossom proceeds (Option A), the execution phase is deferred to post-2052, and 20+ years of additional revenue funds the closure. In that scenario, the NPV of the $900M at 7% over 20 years is approximately $172M — a ~$495M NPV saving on the direct + contingency portion alone (vault file 20, Part C).

- **Low risk** (Targets 1, 4): These are verification/documentation actions or budget-reallocation exercises within existing operational capacity. High confidence in achievement.
