# Speaker Notes — Wallaby Mining Board Presentation
## Solo Presenter Script with Timing (900s / 15 minutes)

**Total time**: 900 seconds (15:00)
**Slides**: 14 main + 6 appendix (appendix not timed — used for Q&A backup)
**Presenter**: Solo graduate challenge

---

### Slide 1 — Title & Agenda (0:00–0:30 | 30s)

> "Good morning. My presentation covers the Wallaby Mining portfolio strategy — specifically the Brave Blossom development decision, our closure liability reduction program, and carbon abatement. Over fifteen minutes I'll walk through three recommendations, the financial case, and the risks. I'll hold questions to the end."

**Timing cue**: Don't rush. Establish authority. Make eye contact with each board member.

---

### Slide 2 — Executive Summary: Three Recommendations (0:30–1:45 | 75s)

> "Three recommendations frame this presentation.
>
> First: proceed with Brave Blossom through a stage-gated funding model. The standalone NPV is $997 million over a 20-year mine life, bringing combined portfolio NPV to $1.28 billion. Capital is $389 million direct — $674 million with 30% contingency — phased across six years with stage gates at each capital commitment point.
>
> Second: actively reduce the $900 million closure liability through six SMART measures, targeting $180.3 million or 20% reduction by end of 2027.
>
> Third: implement carbon abatement — the Safeguard Mechanism baseline is a hard ceiling and 20.1 million tonnes of CO₂ equivalent sit above it. Decommissioned emissions of 4.68 million tonnes represent an ACCU revenue opportunity.
>
> Every dollar invested in Brave Blossom defers $2.31 of closure liability. The net positive NPV of that deferral alone is $185 million before counting any Brave Blossom revenue."

**Timing cue**: 75 seconds — this is the most important slide. Hit the three numbers: $997M, $180.3M, 20.1Mt CO₂e. Pause after "$1.28 billion combined."

---

### Slide 3 — Brave Blossom Valuation (1:45–3:15 | 90s)

> "The financial model shows Brave Blossom generates a standalone NPV of $997 million over a 20-year mine life, bringing combined NPV to $1.28 billion. Capital is $389 million, phased across six years with stage gates.
>
> The IRR is approximately 52% — corrected with the full capital schedule including 30% contingency and tax shield. The earlier 77.4% figure was computed with zero capital, which was a workbook error we identified and corrected in Phase 1.
>
> Critically, the capital-to-liability ratio is 0.43:1 — every dollar invested defers $2.31 of closure liability, yielding a net positive NPV of $185 million before we even count Brave Blossom revenue. The scenario range is $859 million to $1,072 million — all scenarios remain positive.
>
> Sensitivity is driven by realised price and carbon cost — both flagged for board attention in the appendix."

**Timing cue**: Don't dwell on the IRR correction — state it factually and move on. Emphasise "all scenarios positive."

---

### Slide 4 — SWOT Summary (3:15–4:30 | 75s)

> "The SWOT distils to eleven analysis areas across five documents. The key strengths: proven coal quality at 12% discount to benchmark, existing rail and port infrastructure with expansion capacity, and a 20-year mine life providing long revenue tail.
>
> Key weaknesses: the 12% quality discount itself, the $389 million capital intensity relative to Springbok's declining cashflow, and the two-year workforce transition gap.
>
> Opportunities: closure liability deferral is the strategic core — $508 million NPV of deferral exceeds the $323 million present value of capital by $185 million. Carbon abatement has ACCU revenue potential.
>
> Threats: federal approval uncertainty on the Mineral Development Licence, Safeguard Mechanism compliance costs, and thermal-coking coal price volatility. Each is addressed in the risk register and mitigation plan."

**Timing cue**: 75 seconds — hit strengths and opportunities hard, acknowledge weaknesses briefly, threats get one sentence each.

---

### Slide 5 — Capital-to-Liability Analysis (4:30–6:00 | 90s)

> "This is the strategic core of the recommendation. $389 million of capital defers $900 million of closure liability — a 0.43 ratio, or $2.31 deferred per dollar invested.
>
> The NPV of that deferral alone is $508 million at a 7% discount rate over 20 years. That exceeds the $323 million present value of capital at 8% by $185 million — before any Brave Blossom revenue.
>
> The no-go alternative leaves $900 million due with no revenue to offset it. The phased alternative defers the decision but loses the rail renewal window in 2027 and increases capital cost with inflation.
>
> Sensitivity is driven by realised price and carbon cost — both flagged for board attention. The downside case at 75% of base NPV still yields $748 million — still strongly positive."

**Timing cue**: 90 seconds — this slide justifies the entire recommendation. Speak slowly. Let "$2.31 for every dollar" sink in.

---

### Slide 6 — Stage-Gated Funding Model (6:00–7:15 | 75s)

> "We recommend a three-stage funding model. Stage 1: $20 million for studies — PFS drilling, environmental approvals, and frontend engineering. Stage 2: $150 million for early works — CHPP construction, rail spur, and box cut. Stage 3: $219 million for full development.
>
> Each stage has a gate: Stage 1 outputs are a PFS report, federal approval status, and updated capital estimate. Stage 2 outputs are CHPP tender, rail agreement, and first coal timeline. Stage 3 is the full investment decision.
>
> If any gate fails, we exit with sunk costs only — $20 million at Stage 1, $170 million at Stage 2. The $900 million closure liability remains but is not increased. This is the risk-managed path forward."

**Timing cue**: 75 seconds — emphasise the gates. The board's key concern is capital commitment without exit options.

---

### Slide 7 — SMART Closure Reduction (7:15–8:30 | 75s)

> "Six SMART measures target $180.3 million or 20% reduction from the $900 million base.
>
> One: remove duplicate TSF cost — $39.5 to 43.8 million. The Tailings Storage Facility is double-counted across rehabilitation and decommissioning domains.
>
> Two: reduce contingency from 35% to 25% — $39.6 million. Independent quantity surveyor review supports this.
>
> Three: optimise earthworks sequencing — $32 million. Concurrent backfilling reduces rehandle.
>
> Four: infrastructure repurposing for Dustyroo Flats — $11.8 million. 505 houses avoid demolition.
>
> Five: negotiate holding cost reduction — $27 million. Bond structure optimisation.
>
> Six: decommissioning scope refinement — $30.5 million. Plant salvage credits.
>
> Total: $180.3 million. Revised closure estimate: $719.7 million."

**Timing cue**: 75 seconds — don't read every number. Hit the top three: TSF duplicate, contingency, earthworks. Say "total $180.3 million" clearly.

---

### Slide 8 — Carbon & Safeguard (8:30–9:45 | 75s)

> "The Safeguard Mechanism baseline is a hard ceiling. Brave Blossom's 20.1 million tonnes of CO₂ equivalent sit above the baseline. The carbon cost is embedded in the DCF at approximately $200 million over mine life.
>
> However, the decommissioned Springbok operation presents 4.68 million tonnes of emissions that can be abated through methane capture — a dual-purpose investment that generates ACCU revenue and reduces baseline emissions.
>
> We recommend a 70% abatement target using gas capture technology. The ACCU revenue potential offsets a portion of the carbon liability. This is an opportunity, not just a cost."

**Timing cue**: 75 seconds — don't get bogged down in Safeguard arithmetic. Frame it as opportunity (ACCU revenue) not just cost.

---

### Slide 9 — Risk Register (9:45–10:45 | 60s)

> "The top five risks. Federal approval: 25% probability of MDL delay, mitigated by the stage-gated model — Stage 1 is only $20 million. Coal price: 12% discount to benchmark, sensitivity shows ±$80 million NPV per 5% price shift. Carbon: policy volatility, mitigated by abatement investment. Closure estimate: $900 million base could move, mitigated by independent QS review. Workforce: 2-year gap between Springbok and Brave Blossom, mitigated by cross-training.
>
> Traffic lights: Brave Blossom is medium risk, closure reduction is achievable, carbon is medium. All have mitigation plans and are monitored through the stage gates."

**Timing cue**: 60 seconds — this is a speed slide. Don't elaborate. State risk, probability, mitigation, move on.

---

### Slide 10 — Infrastructure & CHPP (10:45–11:45 | 60s)

> "The CHPP has 8 million tonne per year design capacity, with a 68% yield producing 5.4 million tonnes of saleable coal. Capital is $24 million for EPCM delivery. The existing rail and port infrastructure has capacity for 4 million tonnes per annum, expandable to 6 million tonnes.
>
> The rail renewal deadline is 2027 — this aligns with our Stage 1 timeline. If we miss this window, the capital cost increases significantly. This is a timing-critical decision, not just a financial one."

**Timing cue**: 60 seconds — emphasise the 2027 rail renewal deadline. This is the urgency argument.

---

### Slide 11 — Timeline & Milestones (11:45–12:45 | 60s)

> "The timeline: studies commence 2027, Springbok reaches end of life Q4 2031, first Brave Blossom coal in approximately 2033. The gap between Springbok closure and Brave Blossom first revenue is approximately 18 months — the workforce transition and cashflow bridge are addressed in the appendix.
>
> Three stage gates: Gate 1 in late 2027 after PFS and federal approval status. Gate 2 in 2029 after CHPP tender and rail agreement. Gate 3 in 2031 after full development decision.
>
> The 2027 rail renewal deadline drives the urgency — if we don't commit to Stage 1 this year, we lose the rail capacity and the timeline shifts by 2-3 years."

**Timing cue**: 60 seconds — point to the timeline visual. Let the board see the gap between 2031 and 2033.

---

### Slide 12 — Options Comparison (12:45–13:30 | 45s)

> "Three options. Option A: Go — $389 million capital, $997 million standalone NPV, $1.28 billion combined, $180.3 million closure reduction. Option B: No-Go — zero capital, zero revenue, $900 million liability due with no offset. Option C: Phased — defers decision but loses rail window, increases capital with inflation, and doesn't address the closure liability.
>
> Option A dominates on every metric. The stage-gated model manages the downside risk."

**Timing cue**: 45 seconds — this is a quick comparison. Point to the table. Let the numbers speak.

---

### Slide 13 — Social Licence & Community (13:30–14:15 | 45s)

> "Dustyroof Flats: 505 houses. Our infrastructure repurposing plan avoids $11.8 million in demolition costs and provides community assets — the CHPP site becomes a light industrial estate, the rail spur supports a logistics hub.
>
> Workforce: approximately 800 jobs. The 2-year gap is managed through cross-training programs and the owner-operator decision at Stage 1. Social licence is not just compliance — it's a competitive advantage in the next approval cycle."

**Timing cue**: 45 seconds — keep it brief. Social licence is important but not the headline.

---

### Slide 14 — Recommendation Summary (14:15–15:00 | 45s)

> "To summarise: we recommend proceeding with Brave Blossom through stage gates, reducing closure liability by $180.3 million, and implementing carbon abatement. Combined NPV is $1.28 billion. We ask the board to approve $20 million in studies funding, endorse the closure reduction program, and support carbon abatement design.
>
> Every dollar invested defers $2.31 of closure liability. The net positive NPV is $185 million before any Brave Blossom revenue. The downside case is still $748 million.
>
> Thank you. I welcome your questions — appendix slides are available for deeper discussion."

**Timing cue**: 45 seconds — close strong. "Thank you" and stop. Don't trail off.

## Rehearsal Notes

1. **Practice the transitions** — each slide should flow naturally into the next. The key transition is Slide 5 → 6: "Given the capital-to-liability ratio, how do we manage the capital commitment?" → Stage-gated model.
2. **Timing benchmarks**: If you're at Slide 5 at 4:30, you're on pace. If you're at Slide 9 at 9:45, you're on pace. Check your watch at Slides 5, 9, and 12.
3. **Slow down on Slide 5** — this is the strategic core. Most presenters rush it.
4. **Don't memorise verbatim** — know the key numbers and the narrative arc. The script is a guide, not a constraint.
5. **Q&A**: 5 minutes = 3-4 questions. Use appendix slides as visual backup. For hostile questions, use Acknowledge → Reframe → Evidence → Close.
