## SLIDE 1 — Title & Agenda (30s, 0:00–0:30)

> "Good morning. Today I'm presenting the Wallaby Mining Brave Blossom project — a strategic development opportunity that addresses three challenges simultaneously: extending Springbok's mine life, reducing our $900 million closure liability, and positioning the company for a carbon-constrained future. Over the next 15 minutes, I'll cover the strategic context, financial model, risk framework, and three specific recommendations for board approval. I'll hold questions for the end."

**Key**: Confident, measured pace. Establish authority. Don't rush — this sets the tone.

---

## SLIDE 2 — Executive Summary (75s, 0:30–1:45)

> "Three recommendations. First: proceed with Brave Blossom through a stage-gated funding model — $20 million for Stage 1 studies, with full capital of $389 million gated on approval and PFS outcomes. Second: actively reduce the $900 million closure liability by $180.3 million — 20% — through six SMART measures by end of 2027. Third: implement carbon abatement targeting a 70% emissions reduction.
>
> The financial case is compelling. Brave Blossom generates a standalone NPV of $997 million over a 20-year mine life, bringing combined NPV to $1.28 billion. The IRR is approximately 52% — corrected with a fully populated capital schedule. Critically, every dollar invested in Brave Blossom defers $2.31 of closure liability, yielding a net positive NPV of $185 million before we even count Brave Blossom revenue. All scenarios remain positive, ranging from $859 million to $1,072 million."

**Key**: Three clear recommendations → headline numbers. Slow down on the NPV figures — let them land.

---

## SLIDE 3 — Strategic Context (65s, 1:45–2:50)

> "Springbok reaches end-of-life in 2031, with full closure costs of $900 million due from 2032. Without Brave Blossom, Wallaby Mining faces a $900 million outflow with no offsetting revenue. Brave Blossom extends production from 2033 to 2052, generating revenue that funds closure and maintains workforce continuity.
>
> The strategic context is defined by three converging pressures: declining Springbok reserves, tightening carbon regulation under the Safeguard Mechanism, and $900 million in statutory closure obligations. Brave Blossom is not just a new mine — it's the mechanism that makes closure manageable."

**Key**: Frame the problem before the solution. "Without Brave Blossom" is the key phrase.

---

## SLIDE 4 — Project Overview (65s, 2:50–3:55)

> "Brave Blossom is a 20-year mine development adjacent to Springbok, leveraging existing infrastructure — rail, port, CHPP, and workforce. Production targets 8 million tonnes per annum of semi-soft coking coal at $211.20 per tonne. Capital is $389 million direct, or $673.9 million with 30% contingency, phased across six years with three stage gates.
>
> The project sits on MDL 6247, which is currently being converted to a mining lease. We've applied a 25% probability weighting to the federal approval, which is conservative — the deposit is well-characterised from exploration drilling, and the key risk is timing, not geology."

**Key**: Emphasise infrastructure leverage — this is not a greenfield project. "Existing rail, port, CHPP" is the key phrase.

---

## SLIDE 5 — Capital-to-Liability Analysis (75s, 3:55–5:10)

> "This is the strategic core of the presentation. $389 million of capital defers $900 million of closure liability — a ratio of 0.43, or $2.31 deferred for every dollar invested. The NPV of that deferral alone, at a 7% discount rate over 20 years, is $508 million. This exceeds the present value of the capital — $322.9 million at 8% — by approximately $185 million. That's $185 million of positive NPV before we count a single dollar of Brave Blossom revenue.
>
> The no-go alternative leaves $900 million due with no revenue to offset it. This is not a choice between investing and not investing — it's a choice between investing with revenue support and paying full costs with nothing."

**Key**: This is the most important slide. Pause after "$185 million of positive NPV before revenue." Let the board absorb it.

---

## SLIDE 6 — SWOT Summary (65s, 5:10–6:15)

> "The SWOT analysis identifies 11 key areas. Core strengths: existing infrastructure leverage, proven resource, strong financial returns even with full capital schedule. Core weaknesses: concept-stage estimation, federal approval uncertainty, 25% probability weighting on MDL conversion.
>
> The most significant threat is federal approval timing — if MDL conversion takes longer than expected, first coal slips beyond 2033 and the closure gap widens. The key opportunity is the capital-to-liability ratio, which means even a fallback scenario at 75% probability yields $748 million NPV — still strongly positive."

**Key**: Don't read every SWOT item — highlight the top 2 in each quadrant. "Still strongly positive" is the fallback message.

---

## SLIDE 7 — SMART Closure Reduction (65s, 6:15–7:20)

> "Six SMART closure reduction measures target $180.3 million — 20% of the $900 million base liability. These include: removing a duplicated TSF line item worth $39.5 to $43.8 million, reducing contingency from 35% to 25% for $42 million, optimising earthworks through value engineering, deferring non-critical rehabilitation, negotiating shared infrastructure costs, and indexing for inflation more precisely.
>
> These are achievable by end of 2027, subject to independent QS review. The target is 20%, not 18% — we've corrected the earlier draft figure upward based on the detailed line-item audit."

**Key**: "20% by end of 2027" is the commitment. Mention independent QS review — shows rigour.

---

## SLIDE 8 — Carbon & Environment (65s, 7:20–8:25)

> "Carbon liability is embedded in the financial model. Brave Blossom's emissions are 20.1 million tonnes of CO₂ equivalent above the Safeguard Mechanism baseline, costing approximately $200 million over the mine life — already in the DCF. The 4.68 million tonnes from decommissioned mine sites are accounted separately under NGER Method 3.32.
>
> Our carbon abatement strategy targets a 70% emissions reduction through methane capture for power generation — dual-purpose infrastructure that also generates ACCU revenue. This is not a cost centre — it's a revenue-generating compliance strategy."

**Key**: "Already in the DCF" — pre-empts the question. "Revenue-generating compliance" reframes carbon as opportunity.

---

## SLIDE 9 — Stage-Gate Funding Model (65s, 8:25–9:30)

> "The funding model has three stage gates. Stage 1: $20 million for prefeasibility and resource definition drilling — this is what we're asking the board to approve today. Stage 2: $104 million for development, gated on PFS outcomes and MDL approval. Stage 3: $266 million for equipment and commissioning, gated on offtake commitments and final capital approval.
>
> At each gate, the project can be paused or terminated with sunk costs limited to the prior stage. The maximum exposure before full commitment is $124 million — and that only flows if Stage 1 outcomes are positive."

**Key**: "$20 million today" is the ask. Stage gates limit downside — emphasise the "pause or terminate" optionality.

---

## SLIDE 10 — Infrastructure & CHPP (55s, 9:30–10:25)

> "Brave Blossom leverages Springbok's existing infrastructure. The CHPP has 8 million tonnes per annum design capacity with a 68% yield, reaching end-of-life in 2032 — precisely when Brave Blossom begins. We're evaluating refurbishment vs. new build, with the $24 million EPCM estimate favouring refurbishment.
>
> The key decision is owner-operator versus contractor mining, deferred to Stage 1. What matters is that we're not building greenfield — we're extending the life of existing assets that would otherwise be decommissioned at significant cost."

**Key**: "Extending life of existing assets" — this is the efficiency argument. Don't over-promise on CHPP — "evaluating refurbishment vs. new build."

---

## SLIDE 11 — Rail, Port & Workforce (55s, 10:25–11:20)

> "Rail capacity is currently 4 million tonnes per annum, expandable to 6 million with the renewal deadline in 2027. Two competing mines share the corridor, but our existing access agreements give us priority sequencing. Port capacity at Abbot Point is contracted through 2045.
>
> Workforce: approximately 800 jobs transition from Springbok to Brave Blossom with a 2-year gap. The owner-operator decision in Stage 1 determines whether we retain the workforce through the gap or rely on contractor mobilisation. Social licence is anchored in the Dustyroo Flats community — 505 houses, with $11.8 million in demolition costs avoided if the town transitions to Brave Blossom support rather than decommissioning."

**Key**: "Existing access agreements" and "$11.8 million avoided" — quantified community benefit.

---

## SLIDE 12 — Risks & Mitigations (55s, 11:20–12:15)

> "Five key risk categories. Federal approval: mitigated by stage-gating and 25% probability weighting. Coal price: mitigated by semi-soft coking coal positioning and sensitivity testing — even at downside pricing, NPV remains positive. Carbon policy: mitigated by embedding carbon costs in the DCF and dual-purpose abatement. Closure cost inflation: mitigated by SMART reductions and indexation review. Capital overrun: mitigated by 30% contingency and stage-gate controls.
>
> No single risk is project-killing. The combination of stage gates, sensitivity testing, and embedded costs means the project remains robust across all scenarios."

**Key**: "No single risk is project-killing" — this is the confidence statement. Don't minimise risks — acknowledge and mitigate.

---

## SLIDE 13 — Financial Summary (65s, 12:15–13:20)

> "The corrected financial model, with a fully populated capital schedule, shows: Brave Blossom standalone NPV of $997 million at an 8% discount rate. Combined NPV of $1.28 billion including Springbok. IRR of approximately 52% — corrected from the earlier 77.4% figure, which was computed with zero capital. The scenario range is $859 million to $1,072 million — all positive.
>
> Capital is $389 million direct, $518.4 million as scheduled, or $673.9 million with 30% contingency. Peak capital exposure is $221.5 million in 2032. Cumulative DCF turns positive in 2032 at approximately $295 million in the combined scenario. The project is financially robust under all tested scenarios."

**Key**: Be transparent about the IRR correction — "corrected from 77.4%, which was computed with zero capital." Honesty builds credibility. "All positive" is the close.

## SLIDE 14 — Recommendation & Call to Action (40s, 13:20–14:00)

We ask the board to approve $20 million in Stage 1 studies funding, endorse the closure reduction program, and support carbon abatement design.
>
> Every dollar invested defers $2.31 of closure liability. The project is financially robust, strategically necessary, and stage-gated for risk management. I welcome your questions."

## Timing Tips for Solo Presenter

1. **Don't rush Slide 1** — 30s feels short but setting the tone is worth it.
2. **Slide 5 is the anchor** — if you're running long, cut from Slide 10/11, not Slide 5.
3. **Practice the transitions** — the pause between slides should be 1-2 seconds, not 5.
4. **If running over**: Trim Slide 6 (SWOT) to 45s — just hit the top strengths and top threat.
5. **If running under**: Add context to Slide 9 (stage gates) — explain the decision criteria at each gate.
6. **Q&A**: 5 minutes = 3-4 questions. Start with the headline number, then context. Use appendix slides as visual backup.
7. **Hostile questions**: Use Acknowledge → Reframe → Evidence → Close (see Q&A prep document Task 7.5).
